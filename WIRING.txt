================================================================================
SIDETONE / HEADPHONE OUTPUT (PWM on GPIO 12, Pin 16)
================================================================================

No amplifier needed — headphones need only microwatts.

  GPIO12 ──[ 100 Ω ]──┬──[ 10 µF ]──[ 220 Ω ]──► 3.5 mm jack  Tip + Ring
                      │                (+ to GPIO side)
                   [ 100 nF ]
                      │
                     GND ────────────────────────► 3.5 mm jack  Sleeve

    100 Ω / 100 nF   : RC low-pass, fc ≈ 16 kHz, removes the 244 kHz PWM carrier
    10 µF            : DC blocking (output idles at 1.65 V)
    220 Ω            : level + short-circuit protection (≈ 0.7 mW into 32 Ω,
                       already very loud — run the firmware volume at 10–30 %)
    Tip + Ring joined: mono on both ears

    Firmware: keyer tone <300..1200>   keyer vol <0..100>   keyer test 1|0
    The tone follows the on-device keyer and the TUNE carrier.

================================================================================
MICROPHONE (MAX4466 electret module, ADC0 on GPIO 26 / Pin 31)
================================================================================

    MAX4466 VCC ──► 3V3(OUT)  (Pin 36)
    MAX4466 GND ──► GND
    MAX4466 OUT ──► GPIO 26   (Pin 31)   ADC0

    Output idles at VCC/2 ≈ 1.65 V; the firmware removes the DC. Sampled
    at exactly 8 kHz by the ADC itself (48 MHz / 6000) and moved by DMA.
    Set the module's trim pot to roughly mid-travel, then adjust with
    "mic gain <1..50>" and "mic gate <0..0.5>". Select with "src mic",
    or via the OLED MODE item (USB PC -> USB MIC -> CW).

    Without a microphone connected leave src=pc: an open ADC input is noise.

================================================================================
SSB PUSH-TO-TALK — the CW paddle
================================================================================

    No extra wiring: the paddle on GPIO 9 / GPIO 11 doubles as the PTT.
    Either contact works (dit, dah, or both), and a straight key on the tip
    works too.

    In SSB the keyer is inactive, so the paddle has no other job there.

    SSB has no PTT of its own — the carrier follows the audio amplitude. With
    a live microphone that is permanent VOX, so with src=mic the firmware
    requires a paddle contact to be held ("ptt 1", the default). The OLED TX
    field shows "PTT" while it is holding transmission back.

    "ptt 0" disables the requirement and restores the old VOX behaviour.
    PC audio (src=pc) is never gated, so WSJT-X and similar keep working.

================================================================================
CW KEY / PADDLE JACK (3.5 mm stereo)
================================================================================

    Tip    ──► GPIO 9   (Pin 12)   dit
    Ring   ──► GPIO 11  (Pin 15)   dah
    Sleeve ──► GND

    Internal pull-ups, active LOW. A straight key on Tip works in
    "keyer mode straight" (either contact keys).
    In SSB the same contacts act as the push-to-talk — see above.

================================================================================
RECEIVER: Si4732-A10 (QO-100 downlink via LNB + mixer)
================================================================================

Verified operating arrangement (not the older proposed filter/amplifier chain):

    dish -> Bullseye LNB -> triplexer/bias-T -> RMS-11X -> Si4732 pin 8 AMI
                                                 ^
                       TinySA HIGH, 720.137 MHz, +7 dBm, unmodulated LO

There was NO IF LOW-PASS in the setup that received satellite signals.
SAW filters, a 3 dB pad, a proposed IF low-pass and an IF amplifier are separate
experiments, not established prerequisites for that reception. See
HANDOVER_PEGEL.md for measurements and limits of the current beacon identification.
An LTE-800 filter needs a measured passband at 19-20 MHz before use after the mixer.

The optional internal mixer LO is SI5351 CLK0 at 144 MHz; the mixer uses its
5th harmonic at 720 MHz. Its reported -9 dBm level is not equivalent to the
external TinySA's +7 dBm. In the successful 32768 Hz reference configuration
this internal LO is OFF; an external LO is required for conversion.

Si4732-A10 pin assignment (16-pin SOP):

    1  LOUT/DFS        16  ROUT/DOUT
    2  GPO3/DCLK       15  GND
    3  GPO2/INTB       14  VDD
    4  GPO1            13  RCLK
    5  NC              12  SDIO
    6  FMI             11  SCLK
    7  RFGND           10  SENB
    8  AMI              9  RST

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROL BUS -- I2C0 shared with the SI5351
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Si4732  12  SDIO  <---> GPIO 0   (Pin 1)
    Si4732  11  SCLK  <---- GPIO 1   (Pin 2)
    Si4732   9  RST   <---- GPIO 13  (Pin 17)
    Si4732  10  SENB  ----  strap for the intended I2C address

The driver probes 0x11 and 0x63. 0x11 is the observed address here.
The GPO1/GPO2 control-bus straps are sampled at reset; retain the validated
2-wire setup (GPO1/GPO2 open on this build). Ordinary UI changes do not
change those hardware straps.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REFERENCE CLOCK -- successful SSB configuration: true 32768 Hz on CLK2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    SI5351 CLK2 --------------------> Si4732 13 RCLK
    rclk freq 32k                      pin 2 (GPO3) stays open

This is the functional connection, not a claim about the exact current coupling
components. A 1 kOhm / 2.2 nF RCLK RC was tried; noise/wobble remained.
Record the actual clock wiring and measured waveform before further changes.

The successful external reference is 32768 Hz with prescaler 1. The earlier
36 MHz / 1125 = 32000 Hz configuration received AM but not the injected carrier
in SSB. The internal reason is unresolved; "the patch ignores REFCLK_FREQ" is
a hypothesis, not a demonstrated device requirement.

    rclk freq 25  : CLK2 25 MHz, /800 -> 31250 Hz; PLL B 864 MHz
    rclk freq 36  : CLK2 36 MHz, /1125 -> 32000 Hz; PLL B 864 MHz
    rclk freq 32k : CLK2 32768 Hz, /1; PLL B 786.432 MHz; CLK0 OFF
    rclk freq 32m : CLK2 32.768 MHz, /1000; PLL B 864 MHz; CLK0 possible

"32m" is available for investigation; successful SSB reception with it is not
documented here. PLL A and CLK1 remain the transmitter's 52 MHz source.
Moving CLK2 onto PLL A is a proposed separate change, not implemented here.
"rx am"/"rx usb"/"rx on" enable the reference when needed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RF INPUT AND GROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    mixer IF out --------------------> Si4732 8 AMI
    Si4732 7 RFGND -------------------> local RF ground return
    Si4732 6 FMI ---------------------> leave open

AMI is the AM/SSB input; FMI is not used for this IF. Keep the RF lead short
and give RF/audio local ground returns a common reference without routing
digital switching currents through them. A weak reaction to ANTCAP does not
prove a failed input or a need for a resonant transformer. Any added coupling,
termination or filter must be documented and measured in its actual circuit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AUDIO TO THE PICO -- LOUT -> biased ADC1 -> monitor -> headphone PWM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                             3V3 (Pin 36)
                                  |
                              [ 10 kOhm ]
                                  |
    Si4732 1 LOUT --[ 10 uF ]------o--[ 1 kOhm ]--o--> GPIO27 (Pin 32), ADC1
                                  |             |
                              [ 10 kOhm ]    [ 47 nF ]
                                  |             |
                                 GND           GND

    Si4732 16 ROUT remains open (AM/SSB audio is mono).
    Pico AGND is physical pin 33, the analog ground reference.

The divider should give approximately 1.65 V DC. The 10 uF blocks DC.
The originally drawn "+ towards Si4732" is NOT an unconditional polarity rule:
Si47xx LOUT is reported near 0.7 V DC, below the ADC bias. Measure both sides
with respect to ground. A polarized capacitor's positive terminal belongs on
the higher-DC side; alternatively use an appropriate nonpolar capacitor.
No capacitor-polarity correction has yet been proven to cure this build's noise.

1 kOhm / 47 nF is a first-order input low-pass, nominally about 3.4 kHz when
driven by a low-impedance source. The bias/source impedance changes its response;
this is not a high-order anti-alias filter. Keep the ADC/bias wiring short.
The reported strong tone change when touching the divider is a useful symptom,
not proof of one particular reference-clock, supply or grounding fault.

The firmware samples ADC1 at 8 kHz; processing on Core0 removes DC and can apply
a CW audio filter, AGC and limiter. The headphone PWM on GP12 runs at 48 kHz
sample rate with a higher-frequency carrier. RF AGC and this audio AGC are
separate. "rx mon agc 0" sets monitor gain to unity for comparisons.

A local pulse remained with the Si4732 off and the monitor on. In one 19.4 s
comparison RX underruns stayed at 84, ring level about 80/256. That pulse was
not accompanied by new RX ring underruns. "in_peak 0.076" is a software peak
after DC removal, not an independently measured RMS voltage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
POWER AND DIAGNOSTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Si4732 14 VDD <---- 3V3(OUT), Pico Pin 36; 100 nF close to the IC
    Si4732 15 GND ---- local ground return; nearby bulk decoupling
    Si4732  5 NC  ---- leave open

Pin 36 is supplied by the Pico's switching regulator. The firmware defaults
to fixed PWM on the standard Pico 2 via GPIO23; "smps pwm 0|1" compares modes.
This setting does not prove that the analog supply or ADC reference is quiet.
Do not confuse physical pin 23 with GPIO23 (an internal board-control GPIO).

    version                  firmware version, build time and Git ID
    rclk freq 32k            successful reference frequency
    lo 0                     internal mixer LO off; TinySA must supply the LO
    rx usb | lsb | am        receiver mode/start
    rx freq <kHz>            requested IF; decimals allowed; applies rx cal once
    rx cal <Hz>              additive correction, saved to flash
    rx bfo <Hz>              raw BFO override; next rx freq recalculates it
    rx mute 0|1              Si4732 audio mute
    rx mon 0|1               Pico monitor on/off
    rx mon                   monitor diagnostic; clears held peaks, not underruns
    rx mon agc 0|1           Pico audio AGC
    rx mon cw 0              CW audio filter off for general signal tests
    rx mon poll 0|1          periodic Si4732 RSSI readout
    oled freeze 0|1          stop/start display transfers with panel still lit
    oled off | on            panel/charge pump and refresh off/on
    rx | rx diag             requested/configured frequency, cal, BFO, mute, RSQ

Calibration default is -4300 Hz for this device. Valid flash values, including
zero, are preserved. With mixer LO 720.137 MHz, 10489.540 MHz maps to:
    cal 0,     rx freq 19398.7
or: cal -4300, rx freq 19403
These are alternatives. Do not include the LNB error both in the requested IF
and in rx cal. For a known direct IF test source use rx cal 0.

SSB needs the separately fetched patch:
    sh tools/fetch_ssb_patch.sh
CMake reports whether it is present. Patch ID/CTS alone are not RF proof.
The full historical diagnostic handover is in HANDOVER_PEGEL.md.
