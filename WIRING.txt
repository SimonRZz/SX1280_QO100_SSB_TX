================================================================================
SIDETONE / HEADPHONE OUTPUT (PWM on GPIO 12, Pin 16)
================================================================================

No amplifier needed — headphones need only microwatts.

  GPIO12 ──[ 100 Ω ]──┬──[ 10 µF ]──[ 220 Ω ]──► 3.5 mm jack  Tip + Ring
                      │                (+ to GPIO side)
                   [ 100 nF ]
                      │
                     GND ────────────────────────► 3.5 mm jack  Sleeve

    100 Ω / 100 nF   : RC low-pass, fc ≈ 16 kHz, removes the 244 kHz PWM carrier
    10 µF            : DC blocking (output idles at 1.65 V)
    220 Ω            : level + short-circuit protection (≈ 0.7 mW into 32 Ω,
                       already very loud — run the firmware volume at 10–30 %)
    Tip + Ring joined: mono on both ears

    Firmware: keyer tone <300..1200>   keyer vol <0..100>   keyer test 1|0
    The tone follows the on-device keyer and the TUNE carrier.

================================================================================
MICROPHONE (MAX4466 electret module, ADC0 on GPIO 26 / Pin 31)
================================================================================

    MAX4466 VCC ──► 3V3(OUT)  (Pin 36)
    MAX4466 GND ──► GND
    MAX4466 OUT ──► GPIO 26   (Pin 31)   ADC0

    Output idles at VCC/2 ≈ 1.65 V; the firmware removes the DC. Sampled
    at exactly 8 kHz by the ADC itself (48 MHz / 6000) and moved by DMA.
    Set the module's trim pot to roughly mid-travel, then adjust with
    "mic gain <1..50>" and "mic gate <0..0.5>". Select with "src mic",
    or via the OLED MODE item (USB PC -> USB MIC -> CW).

    Without a microphone connected leave src=pc: an open ADC input is noise.

================================================================================
SSB PUSH-TO-TALK — the CW paddle
================================================================================

    No extra wiring: the paddle on GPIO 9 / GPIO 11 doubles as the PTT.
    Either contact works (dit, dah, or both), and a straight key on the tip
    works too.

    In SSB the keyer is inactive, so the paddle has no other job there.

    SSB has no PTT of its own — the carrier follows the audio amplitude. With
    a live microphone that is permanent VOX, so with src=mic the firmware
    requires a paddle contact to be held ("ptt 1", the default). The OLED TX
    field shows "PTT" while it is holding transmission back.

    "ptt 0" disables the requirement and restores the old VOX behaviour.
    PC audio (src=pc) is never gated, so WSJT-X and similar keep working.

================================================================================
CW KEY / PADDLE JACK (3.5 mm stereo)
================================================================================

    Tip    ──► GPIO 9   (Pin 12)   dit
    Ring   ──► GPIO 11  (Pin 15)   dah
    Sleeve ──► GND

    Internal pull-ups, active LOW. A straight key on Tip works in
    "keyer mode straight" (either contact keys).
    In SSB the same contacts act as the push-to-talk — see above.

================================================================================
RECEIVER: Si4732-A10 (QO-100 downlink via LNB + mixer)
================================================================================

Signal chain:

   dish -> Bullseye LNB (LO 9750) -> 739.5 MHz -> triplexer -> SAW filter
        -> RMS-11X mixer -> 19.5 MHz IF -> 3 dB pad -> low-pass
        -> Si4732 (SSB) -> audio -> GPIO 27 (ADC1)

   SI5351 CLK0, 144 MHz -> high-pass -> SPF5189Z -> mixer LO
                                         (5th harmonic, 720 MHz)
   SI5351 CLK2,  25 MHz -> divider -> Si4732 RCLK (pin 13)

Both from the PICO's SI5351, so the receiver needs no second board: the Pro
Micro GPSDO is not part of the receive chain at all.

RMS-11X pins: 1 = LO, 4 = IF, 5 = RF, 2/3/6 = ground. Verify with an ohmmeter
before soldering: pins 2, 3 and 6 read 0 ohm to each other, pins 1 and 5 read a
few ohms to ground through their transformer windings, and pin 4 reads much
higher because it is the diode ring. That identifies IF unambiguously; LO versus
RF needs the package's pin-1 marker. RF/LO cover 5-1900 MHz and IF 5-1000 MHz,
so every frequency here is comfortably in range. Maximum RF input is 200 mW and
the IF port tolerates 40 mA of DC — if the receiver on the IF side has a bias
tee, switch it OFF.

The mixer LO is GPS-disciplined, and so is the Si4732's reference clock, which
takes 25 MHz from the second GPSDO. THE LNB IS NOT: the Bullseye runs on its own
TCXO and cannot accept an external reference, because the 25 MHz on its red
connector is an output. Its +-2 ppm, roughly +-10 kHz at room temperature, is
therefore the accuracy limit of the whole receive side and is calibrated out with
"rx cal" against the beacon.

Si4732-A10 pin assignment (16-pin SOP):

    1  LOUT/DFS        16  ROUT/DOUT
    2  GPO3/DCLK       15  GND
    3  GPO2/INTB       14  VDD
    4  GPO1            13  RCLK
    5  NC              12  SDIO
    6  FMI             11  SCLK
    7  RFGND           10  SENB
    8  AMI              9  RST

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROL BUS — shares I2C0 with the SI5351, no extra pins
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Si4732  12  SDIO  <--->  GPIO 0   (Pin 1)    with the SI5351
    Si4732  11  SCLK  <----  GPIO 1   (Pin 2)    with the SI5351
    Si4732   9  RST   <----  GPIO 13  (Pin 17)
    Si4732  10  SENB  ----   see below

SENB sets the I2C address. The polarity is documented inconsistently between
the Si4732-A10 and the Si4735-D60 that most application notes describe, so the
driver probes BOTH 0x11 and 0x63 and reports which one answered ("rx diag").
Strap SENB either way; 0x11 is preferable because gpsdo.c scans 0x60..0x63 for
the SI5351 and would mistake a device at 0x63 for the clock generator if the
SI5351 ever failed to answer.

Pull-ups are already on the bus for the SI5351 (internal, weak). The patch
upload is 15832 bytes and takes roughly 2-3 s at 100 kHz. External 4.7 kOhm
pull-ups would allow 400 kHz and cut that to well under a second, but the bus
speed is shared with the SI5351 — do not raise it without checking that the
clock generator still configures reliably.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BUS MODE — the one thing a bare chip needs and a module does not
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Si4732   4  GPO1  ----  leave OPEN
    Si4732   3  GPO2  ----  leave OPEN

The chip latches its control-bus mode from GPO1 and GPO2 on the RISING edge of
RST. Left open, the internal pull-up on GPO1 and pull-down on GPO2 select
2-wire (I2C) mode, which is what we want — but only if RST stays low long
enough for them to settle (100 us minimum with the pins high-impedance).

Two further conditions, both handled in si_hw_reset():
  - SCLK must be HIGH across the rising edge of RST. Because the SI5351 shares
    this bus, the driver takes GPIO 1 away from the I2C block and drives it
    high by hand for the duration, otherwise a transfer in flight could leave
    SCL low at exactly the wrong moment and the chip would come up in SPI mode.
  - No I2C start condition in the 300 ns before that edge.

If "rx diag" reports the chip answering but GET_REV failing, the bus mode
latched wrong — that is the first thing to check.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REFERENCE CLOCK — 25 MHz from SI5351 CLK2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    SI5351 CLK2 ──[ 1 nF ]──> Si4732  13  RCLK
    (25 MHz, 8 mA)
                                        pin 2 (GPO3) stays OPEN

NO VOLTAGE DIVIDER. Coupling capacitor only, and drive the output hard.

This was wrong in an earlier version of these notes, which put a 1 kOhm / 220 Ohm
divider here on the strength of a "0.3 to 1.5 V" input range recalled from
AN332. That figure describes a crystal-style input fed with a sine wave, not a
25 MHz CMOS clock. With the divider and 2 mA drive the chip behaved exactly as
if it had no reference at all: it answered on I2C, reported part 32 and firmware
1.0, accepted commands — and refused to tune, returning "tune did not complete"
and 0 kHz read back from AM_TUNE_STATUS. Raising the drive to 8 mA fixed it
immediately: tune complete, correct frequency, real RSSI readings.

Use "rclk drive 2|4|6|8" to set it from the console; 8 mA is the value that
works. The 1 nF rather than 100 pF is for margin — 6 ohms of reactance at
25 MHz instead of 64.

Same SI5351 as everything else, PLL B through a fractional MultiSynth:
864 / (34 + 14/25) = 25.000000 MHz exactly, divided by 800 inside the Si4732 to
31250 Hz. Enable it with "rclk 1"; "rx on" does that for you.

VERIFIED, not assumed: AN332's SSB amendment supports RCLK from 31130 Hz to
40 MHz, so 25 MHz is in range, and 31250 Hz sits inside the 31130..34406 Hz
REFCLK window. Prescaler 800 with RCLKSEL = 0 selects the RCLK pin.

Fractional is fine here. The Si4732 divides the 25 MHz down by 800 and
multiplies back up internally, so the two roughly cancel, and the chip is built
to run off a 32.768 kHz watch crystal at +-20 ppm — its tolerance for reference
jitter is orders of magnitude above the SX1280's. The fraction is exact, so the
frequency is exact.

This used to come from the second GPSDO on the Pro Micro, which was built to
feed the LNB a 25 MHz reference. The Bullseye cannot accept one, so that board
has no job in the receiver any more and is free for the LNB-disciplining idea
instead.

THE THREE OUTPUTS, all on the Pico's SI5351:

    CLK0  144 MHz, 8 mA, PLL B integer      mixer LO         "lo 0|1"
    CLK1   52 MHz, 4 mA, PLL A fractional   SX1280 XTA       never switched
    CLK2   25 MHz, 8 mA, PLL B fractional   Si4732 RCLK      "rclk 0|1"

CLK1 is the SX1280's only clock. si_write_oeb() clears its enable bit
unconditionally, so no combination of "lo" and "rclk" can take it away, and
PLL A must never be reset at runtime. Both receive-side outputs come up OFF:
gpsdo_init() runs before the boot gate that waits for a locked 52 MHz, and
nothing on the receive side may keep the transmitter from starting.

A CHIP THAT TALKS BUT WILL NOT TUNE IS A REFERENCE-CLOCK FAULT. "rx diag" shows
it plainly: part number and firmware come back fine, while "tuner reports 0 kHz,
tune complete=0". Nothing about the signal path can cause that.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF INPUT — AMI, not FMI
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                                  120 nH
    mixer IF out ──[ 3 dB pad ]────(((((((──┬──[ 10 nF ]──> Si4732  8  AMI
                              │             │
                            27 pF         27 pF
                              │             │
                             GND           GND

    Si4732   7  RFGND  ----  GND (keep this separate from digital ground)
    Si4732   6  FMI    ----  leave OPEN

SSB runs through the AM path, so the 19.5 MHz IF goes to AMI. Keep the lead
short. A weak QO-100 signal lands around -80 dBm here, roughly 30 dB above the
receiver's sensitivity, so there is margin — but only behind that low-pass.

THE LOW-PASS IS NOT OPTIONAL. A double balanced mixer leaks its local
oscillator into the IF port; LO-to-IF isolation is typically 20 to 30 dB. With
-5 dBm at the LO port that puts -25 to -35 dBm of 720 MHz on the IF line, which
is 49 to 59 dB STRONGER than the beacon you are trying to hear. Any receiver
with an AGC locks onto that instead, and on an 8-bit SDR the wanted signal
disappears into quantisation noise because the converter only spans about 48 dB.
Measured on the bench: without the filter, nothing below roughly -55 dBm at the
IF was audible, which is a dynamic-range limit, not a sensitivity limit.

    19.5 - 20.0 MHz   0.0 dB     720 MHz (LO leak)    46 dB
    144 MHz           5   dB     739.5 MHz (RF leak)  47 dB

Component values suit a 50 ohm system and are what happened to be in the parts
bin; the design is forgiving. Two 27 pF in parallel with 15 pF each (42 pF)
raises the 720 MHz rejection to 54 dB for the same effort. A 2.2 uH inductor
would put the corner at 7 MHz and kill the IF itself — 120 nH is the right one.

ORDER MATTERS: the pad goes BEFORE the low-pass. That way the mixer sees a
resistive load rather than the filter's reactance, and the filter gets a defined
source impedance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AUDIO INTO THE PICO — GPIO 27 / ADC1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                             3V3 (Pin 36)
                                  │
                              [ 10 kOhm ]
                                  │
    Si4732  1  LOUT ──[ 10 uF ]───┼──[ 1 kOhm ]──> GPIO 27 (Pin 32)  ADC1
                       (+ to          │                  │
                        Si4732)  [ 10 kOhm ]        [ 47 nF ]
                                      │                  │
                                     GND                GND

    Si4732  16  ROUT  ----  leave OPEN (AM/SSB audio is mono)

Same arrangement as the microphone on ADC0: the divider sets the operating
point to 1.65 V, the 10 uF blocks DC. The 1 kOhm with 47 nF is the anti-alias
filter at 3.4 kHz, matched to the 8 kHz sample rate.

USED BY THE FIRMWARE since stage R2a: 'rx mon 1' feeds this into the headphone
jack. The samples are levelled on core 0 (DC removal, AGC, hard limiter) and
mixed into the same PWM stream that carries the sidetone, so NOTHING changes on
the headphone wiring - this one connection is the whole hardware change.

    rx mon [0|1]          receiver into the headphones
    rx mon vol <0..100>   level
    rx mon agc [0|1]      audio AGC, on by default

The AGC turns down fast and comes back slowly, so a station that suddenly booms
in cannot hurt, and a pause between words does not pump. Its range is -26 to
+36 dB and a hard limiter sits after it, because the AGC follows an envelope
and a transient outruns any envelope.

THE ADC IS SHARED. There is one sample-and-hold and one FIFO on the chip, and
the microphone on ADC0 already owns them. Round-robin over both inputs would
interleave them into a single stream whose phase holds only as long as no
sample is ever dropped, and a slipped phase would feed receive audio into the
transmit modulator. So the input is switched instead: while the monitor runs it
has ADC1, and the transmit audio source is forced to PC. 'rx mon 1' says so
when it does that. Microphone monitoring - the third part of this stage - needs
that round-robin and is not built yet.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONSOLE COMMANDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    i2c                  scan both buses; shows what actually answers
    rclk [0|1]           25 MHz on CLK2 -> Si4732 pin 13. OFF at boot.
    lo [0|1]             144 MHz on CLK0 -> mixer LO. OFF at boot.
    rx                   receiver status (!R line)
    rx diag              detailed report, including which address answered
    rx on | off          power up / down. Enables rclk first, then uploads
                         the SSB patch — the chip cannot tune without its
                         reference clock.
    rx cal <Hz>          LNB oscillator error, saved to flash
    rx usb | lsb | am    demodulation mode
    rx freq <kHz>        2300..26100
    rx vol <0..63>
    rx bw <Hz>           nearest available filter
    rx bfo <Hz>          fine tuning, 8 Hz steps
    rx mute 0|1

SSB needs a patch that is not in this repository — run

    sh tools/fetch_ssb_patch.sh

once before building, or the receiver is AM only. CMake says which at configure
time, and "rx usb" refuses with an explanatory error if it is missing.
