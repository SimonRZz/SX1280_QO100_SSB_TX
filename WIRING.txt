================================================================================
SIDETONE / HEADPHONE OUTPUT (PWM on GPIO 12, Pin 16)
================================================================================

No amplifier needed — headphones need only microwatts.

  GPIO12 ──[ 100 Ω ]──┬──[ 10 µF ]──[ 220 Ω ]──► 3.5 mm jack  Tip + Ring
                      │                (+ to GPIO side)
                   [ 100 nF ]
                      │
                     GND ────────────────────────► 3.5 mm jack  Sleeve

    100 Ω / 100 nF   : RC low-pass, fc ≈ 16 kHz, removes the 244 kHz PWM carrier
    10 µF            : DC blocking (output idles at 1.65 V)
    220 Ω            : level + short-circuit protection (≈ 0.7 mW into 32 Ω,
                       already very loud — run the firmware volume at 10–30 %)
    Tip + Ring joined: mono on both ears

    Firmware: keyer tone <300..1200>   keyer vol <0..100>   keyer test 1|0
    The tone follows the on-device keyer and the TUNE carrier.

================================================================================
MICROPHONE (MAX4466 electret module, ADC0 on GPIO 26 / Pin 31)
================================================================================

    MAX4466 VCC ──► 3V3(OUT)  (Pin 36)
    MAX4466 GND ──► GND
    MAX4466 OUT ──► GPIO 26   (Pin 31)   ADC0

    Output idles at VCC/2 ≈ 1.65 V; the firmware removes the DC. Sampled
    at exactly 8 kHz by the ADC itself (48 MHz / 6000) and moved by DMA.
    Set the module's trim pot to roughly mid-travel, then adjust with
    "mic gain <1..50>" and "mic gate <0..0.5>". Select with "src mic",
    or via the OLED MODE item (USB PC -> USB MIC -> CW).

    Without a microphone connected leave src=pc: an open ADC input is noise.

================================================================================
SSB PUSH-TO-TALK — the CW paddle
================================================================================

    No extra wiring: the paddle on GPIO 9 / GPIO 11 doubles as the PTT.
    Either contact works (dit, dah, or both), and a straight key on the tip
    works too.

    In SSB the keyer is inactive, so the paddle has no other job there.

    SSB has no PTT of its own — the carrier follows the audio amplitude. With
    a live microphone that is permanent VOX, so with src=mic the firmware
    requires a paddle contact to be held ("ptt 1", the default). The OLED TX
    field shows "PTT" while it is holding transmission back.

    "ptt 0" disables the requirement and restores the old VOX behaviour.
    PC audio (src=pc) is never gated, so WSJT-X and similar keep working.

================================================================================
CW KEY / PADDLE JACK (3.5 mm stereo)
================================================================================

    Tip    ──► GPIO 9   (Pin 12)   dit
    Ring   ──► GPIO 11  (Pin 15)   dah
    Sleeve ──► GND

    Internal pull-ups, active LOW. A straight key on Tip works in
    "keyer mode straight" (either contact keys).
    In SSB the same contacts act as the push-to-talk — see above.

================================================================================
RECEIVER: Si4732-A10 (QO-100 downlink via LNB + mixer)
================================================================================

Signal chain:

   dish -> LNB (LO 9750) -> 739.5 MHz -> bandpass -> RMS-11X mixer
        -> 19.5 MHz IF -> Si4732 (SSB) -> audio -> GPIO 27 (ADC1)

The mixer LO is the 5th harmonic of 144 MHz from SI5351 CLK0; the LNB reference
and the Si4732 reference both come from the second GPSDO. The whole receive
chain therefore hangs off GPS, same as the transmitter.

Si4732-A10 pin assignment (16-pin SOP):

    1  LOUT/DFS        16  ROUT/DOUT
    2  GPO3/DCLK       15  GND
    3  GPO2/INTB       14  VDD
    4  GPO1            13  RCLK
    5  NC              12  SDIO
    6  FMI             11  SCLK
    7  RFGND           10  SENB
    8  AMI              9  RST

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROL BUS — shares I2C0 with the SI5351, no extra pins
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Si4732  12  SDIO  <--->  GPIO 0   (Pin 1)    with the SI5351
    Si4732  11  SCLK  <----  GPIO 1   (Pin 2)    with the SI5351
    Si4732   9  RST   <----  GPIO 13  (Pin 17)
    Si4732  10  SENB  ----   see below

SENB sets the I2C address. The polarity is documented inconsistently between
the Si4732-A10 and the Si4735-D60 that most application notes describe, so the
driver probes BOTH 0x11 and 0x63 and reports which one answered ("rx diag").
Strap SENB either way; 0x11 is preferable because gpsdo.c scans 0x60..0x63 for
the SI5351 and would mistake a device at 0x63 for the clock generator if the
SI5351 ever failed to answer.

Pull-ups are already on the bus for the SI5351 (internal, weak). The patch
upload is 15832 bytes and takes roughly 2-3 s at 100 kHz. External 4.7 kOhm
pull-ups would allow 400 kHz and cut that to well under a second, but the bus
speed is shared with the SI5351 — do not raise it without checking that the
clock generator still configures reliably.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BUS MODE — the one thing a bare chip needs and a module does not
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Si4732   4  GPO1  ----  leave OPEN
    Si4732   3  GPO2  ----  leave OPEN

The chip latches its control-bus mode from GPO1 and GPO2 on the RISING edge of
RST. Left open, the internal pull-up on GPO1 and pull-down on GPO2 select
2-wire (I2C) mode, which is what we want — but only if RST stays low long
enough for them to settle (100 us minimum with the pins high-impedance).

Two further conditions, both handled in si_hw_reset():
  - SCLK must be HIGH across the rising edge of RST. Because the SI5351 shares
    this bus, the driver takes GPIO 1 away from the I2C block and drives it
    high by hand for the duration, otherwise a transfer in flight could leave
    SCL low at exactly the wrong moment and the chip would come up in SPI mode.
  - No I2C start condition in the 300 ns before that edge.

If "rx diag" reports the chip answering but GET_REV failing, the bus mode
latched wrong — that is the first thing to check.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REFERENCE CLOCK — 25 MHz from the second GPSDO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    SI5351 CLK2 ──[ 1 kOhm ]──┬──[ 100 pF ]──> Si4732  13  RCLK
                              │
                          [ 220 Ohm ]              pin 2 (GPO3) stays OPEN
                              │
                             GND

There is no XTAL1/XTAL2 pair on the 16-pin package: RCLK is its own pin, and in
crystal mode the 32.768 kHz crystal would sit between pin 13 and pin 2. We use
neither — the firmware powers up with XOSCEN = 0 and sets
REFCLK_PRESCALE = 800, REFCLK_FREQ = 31250 (25 MHz / 800, exact).

The divider is there because the SI5351 delivers about 2.4 V peak-to-peak at
8 mA drive while the RCLK input wants roughly 0.3 to 1.5 V. 1 kOhm / 220 Ohm
gives about 0.44 V. Set CLK2 to 2 mA drive as well for margin.

  NOT VERIFIED: the RCLK input level limits and the 31130..34406 Hz window come
  from AN332 as carried by the PU2CLR library headers. The window is confirmed;
  the input level range is not. Check the data sheet before committing to this
  divider.

Why bother: the 32.768 kHz watch crystal is +-20 ppm, which at a 19.5 MHz IF is
about +-390 Hz. That would have made the automatic coupling to the transmit
frequency useless and wasted the whole GPSDO effort.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF INPUT — AMI, not FMI
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    mixer IF out ──[ 3 dB pad ]──[ 10 nF ]──> Si4732   8  AMI
    Si4732   7  RFGND  ----  GND (keep this separate from digital ground)
    Si4732   6  FMI    ----  leave OPEN

SSB runs through the AM path, so the 19.5 MHz IF goes to AMI. Keep the lead
short. A weak QO-100 signal lands around -74 dBm here, roughly 36 dB above the
receiver's sensitivity, so there is plenty of margin.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AUDIO INTO THE PICO — GPIO 27 / ADC1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                             3V3 (Pin 36)
                                  │
                              [ 10 kOhm ]
                                  │
    Si4732  1  LOUT ──[ 10 uF ]───┼──[ 1 kOhm ]──> GPIO 27 (Pin 32)  ADC1
                       (+ to          │                  │
                        Si4732)  [ 10 kOhm ]        [ 47 nF ]
                                      │                  │
                                     GND                GND

    Si4732  16  ROUT  ----  leave OPEN (AM/SSB audio is mono)

Same arrangement as the microphone on ADC0: the divider sets the operating
point to 1.65 V, the 10 uF blocks DC. The 1 kOhm with 47 nF is the anti-alias
filter at 3.4 kHz, matched to the 8 kHz sample rate.

Not yet used by the firmware — stage R1 is the driver only, and you listen on
the Si4732's own audio output to prove the receive hardware works. Wiring it up
now does no harm.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
POWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Si4732  14  VDD  <----  3V3(OUT) (Pin 36)   + 100 nF close to the chip
    Si4732  15  GND  ----   GND                 + 10 uF bulk
    Si4732   5  NC   ----   leave OPEN

There is no separate VIO pin on the 16-pin package; VDD supplies both.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONSOLE COMMANDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    rx                   receiver status (!R line)
    rx diag              detailed report, including which address answered
    rx on | off          power up / down (power-up uploads the SSB patch)
    rx usb | lsb | am    demodulation mode
    rx freq <kHz>        2300..26100
    rx vol <0..63>
    rx bw <Hz>           nearest available filter
    rx bfo <Hz>          fine tuning, 8 Hz steps
    rx mute 0|1

SSB needs a patch that is not in this repository — run

    sh tools/fetch_ssb_patch.sh

once before building, or the receiver is AM only. CMake says which at configure
time, and "rx usb" refuses with an explanatory error if it is missing.
